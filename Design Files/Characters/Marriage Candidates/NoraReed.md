# Personality Information

Character Arc: Learning That It's Okay to Weather the Storm

Character Arc Start State: Nora believes dwelling on hardship only makes life heavier. Whenever something painful happens, she'd rather stay busy, crack a joke, or head out on the river than sit with difficult emotions.

Character Arc End State: Nora learns that healing isn't about pretending everything is fine. By allowing herself to face loss and vulnerability, she discovers that sharing burdens with others makes joy even more meaningful.

Core Belief: No matter how rough the waters get, tomorrow is another chance to cast your line.

Core Misbelief: If I stop smiling, I'll only make things harder for everyone else.

Core Traits: Grounded, Independent, Optimistic

Defining Sentence: ”Life’s too short to leave a good story untold.”

Dialogue Length: Long

Emotional Need: To realize that being vulnerable doesn't make her a burden—it allows others to care for her the way she so naturally cares for them.

Favorite Location: At the edge of the River Wharf overlooking the Graythorne River

Fears: Nora fears becoming trapped by grief or regret. She worries that if she allows herself to dwell on painful memories, she'll lose the hopeful outlook that's always carried her forward.

Goals: To make Riverbend Fishery the happiest place in Blackmere, where every visitor leaves with a memorable story and a reason to come back.

Habit: Sings sea shanties while fishing or walking along the river banks.

History: Nora grew up beside the river, learning to read currents, weather, and wildlife long before she could properly cast a fishing line. During the darkest months after the Great Flood, Nora was often the one organizing shared meals, fishing trips, and impromptu competitions simply to give people something to smile about. Her cheerful outlook quickly became contagious, though few realize how much effort it takes to keep that optimism afloat.

Hobbies: Fishing at sunrise; swimming; skipping stones across the river; collecting unusual shells; making up ridiculous sea shanties; teaching children "pirate voices”; telling exaggerated stories about "the one that got away.”

Motivation: Nora wants people to associate the river with hope instead of tragedy, believing that every peaceful day on the water helps Blackmere heal a little more.

Personality Colors: Ocean Blue, Seafoam Green, Gold

Personality Summary: Nora is energetic, quick-witted, and effortlessly charismatic. She has an uncanny talent for turning strangers into friends, quiet moments into stories, and ordinary days into unforgettable memories. Whether she's exaggerating the size of yesterday's catch, organizing an impromptu fishing contest, or making children laugh with ridiculous sea legends, she naturally becomes the center of any gathering. Beneath that laughter, however, is someone who quietly fears that if she ever stops lifting everyone else's spirits, they'll see just how much pain she's still carrying.

Public Persona: Everyone knows Nora as the unofficial heartbeat of the River Wharf. If there's a celebration, she's probably organizing it. If there's an awkward silence, she'll fill it with an outrageous story. If someone's having a bad day, she'll somehow have them laughing before sunset. Most people can't imagine Blackmere without Nora's laughter echoing across the river.

Secret: Nora still avoids one quiet stretch of the river where the floodwaters were at their strongest. She tells everyone it's because the fishing isn't very good there, but the truth is she's never been able to bring herself to return.

Speaking Tone: Playful

Strength: Resilience

Weakness: Avoidance

# Schedule & Routine

## General Routine

Nora follows an energetic, social, and river-centered routine built around Riverbend Fishery, the River Wharf District, and the Graythorne River.

As Blackmere's fisher, Nora begins her working day earlier than most residents. Riverbend Fishery opens at 6:00 AM, so Nora is already awake and working when the game day begins.

Nora spends her mornings operating Riverbend Fishery, maintaining fishing equipment, preparing nets, sorting catches, assisting customers, and completing practical work around the docks.

Riverbend Fishery is both Nora's residence and workplace. She shares the residence with Elise Moreau, her best friend.

The fishery closes to the public at 2:00 PM every day. Nora rarely treats that as the end of her day.

Her afternoons and evenings are much less structured than her mornings. She fishes along the Graythorne River, walks the River Wharf, swims when weather permits, skips stones, collects unusual shells, spends time with friends, organizes informal fishing activities, and tells stories to anyone willing to listen.

The edge of the River Wharf overlooking the Graythorne River is Nora's favorite location.

Nora is extremely sociable and knows many residents throughout Blackmere. Unlike more reserved characters, her schedule deliberately places her in public spaces after work rather than immediately returning home.

Nora's greatest difficulty is allowing quiet moments to remain quiet. Whenever uncomfortable emotions begin to surface, she instinctively finds something to do, someone to entertain, or somewhere else to go.

## Weather Overrides

### Rainy

Nora likes rainy weather and generally follows her normal schedule.

Riverbend Fishery has no rain closure, so Nora continues operating the fishery from 6:00 AM to 2:00 PM.

After work, Nora becomes more likely to fish during ordinary rainfall.

She enjoys:

- Watching rain strike the river
- Fishing from covered or safer areas along the waterfront
- Observing changes in the current
- Singing rain-themed sea shanties
- Walking along the River Wharf in waterproof clothing

Swimming is replaced with fishing or walking during rainy weather.

Nora's mood remains noticeably upbeat during rain.

### Rainy (Severe)

Nora likes severe rain despite the danger associated with it.

Riverbend Fishery has no severe-rain closure, so the fishery remains open from 6:00 AM to 2:00 PM.

However, Nora's behavior changes significantly.

She remains around Riverbend Fishery and the safer portions of the River Wharf rather than taking long recreational trips along the river.

Her schedule emphasizes:

- Checking dock lines
- Securing fishing equipment
- Monitoring the river level
- Moving loose equipment away from the water
- Checking nets and boats
- Helping protect the fishery from damage

Nora does not swim or recreationally fish during dangerous conditions.

Severe rain also makes Nora unusually restless because of her memories of the Great Flood.

She responds by keeping herself extremely busy and making more jokes than usual.

### Snowy

Nora generally follows her normal fishery schedule.

She spends less time fishing after work and more time around Riverbend Fishery, Winding Banks Inn, or other indoor locations.

She does not swim.

Short walks along the River Wharf remain possible when conditions are safe.

### Snowy (Severe)

Nora dislikes severe snow.

Although Riverbend Fishery has no weather closure, Nora limits unnecessary outdoor activity.

After closing the fishery at 2:00 PM, she generally remains at Riverbend Fishery or visits Winding Banks Inn.

She does not fish recreationally, swim, or take long waterfront walks.

### Windy

Nora likes windy weather and generally follows her normal schedule.

She enjoys watching the river become rougher and listening to the wind move across the waterfront.

Fishing locations are adjusted when necessary for safety.

She may spend additional time securing nets and equipment around Riverbend Fishery.

The fishery has no regular closed day and no weather closures.

Nora therefore begins every standard day at Riverbend Fishery when the game day begins at 6:00 AM.

- A story event takes priority
- A relationship event takes priority
- A festival takes priority

This distinguishes her from business owners whose weekly closed days provide their primary schedule variation.

## Routine Characterization

Nora's routine is intentionally divided into two very different halves.

Her mornings are dependable.

Her afternoons are spontaneous.

Riverbend Fishery opens at 6:00 AM, making Nora one of Blackmere's earliest-working residents.

Despite her playful personality, she takes the fishery seriously.

She maintains equipment.

She repairs nets.

She keeps the docks functional.

She assists customers.

She shows up every morning.

This is important because Nora's optimism should never be confused with irresponsibility.

She has high diligence and duty, and Riverbend Fishery matters deeply to her.

Once the fishery closes at 2:00 PM, however, Nora becomes much less predictable.

She may fish.

She may swim.

She may start talking to someone and lose an hour telling stories.

She may organize an informal fishing contest.

She may teach a child a ridiculous pirate voice.

She may walk along the river singing a sea shanty she invented thirty seconds earlier.

This makes Nora's schedule feel more organic than a character who moves through the exact same sequence of professional locations every day.

The River Wharf is particularly important.

Nora's favorite location is specifically the edge of the wharf overlooking the Graythorne River.

That means the river is not merely where she works.

It is where she thinks.

It is where she relaxes.

It is where she socializes.

It is where many of her happiest memories exist.

It is also where some of her worst memories exist.

That contradiction is central to Nora's character.

She wants Blackmere to associate the river with hope rather than tragedy.

She has spent years actively creating happy memories there because she genuinely believes those memories matter.

Fishing contests, stories, shared meals, jokes, swimming, and casual afternoons by the water are not meaningless distractions.

They are part of how Nora helped Blackmere recover.

The problem is that she uses the same strategy on herself.

Whenever something hurts, she creates noise.

She finds someone to talk to.

She makes a joke.

She goes fishing.

She starts singing.

She keeps moving.

That behavior should be visible in her schedule.

Nora spends surprisingly little time completely alone despite being highly independent.

Those qualities are not contradictory.

She does not need other people to function.

She wants other people around because she genuinely loves them and because social activity gives her very little opportunity to sit quietly with herself.

Her relationship with Elise is especially important because they are best friends and housemates.

Their personalities are dramatically different.

Elise is calculating, composed, and disciplined.

Nora is spontaneous, playful, and emotionally expressive on the surface.

That makes Riverbend Fishery an interesting shared home.

Nora can return from the river loudly describing a fish that becomes larger every time she mentions it.

Elise knows perfectly well that half the story is nonsense.

She listens anyway.

Friday gives their friendship a more deliberate place in the schedule.

Nora meets Elise after the bank closes, and they spend the evening together away from their respective jobs.

The relationship should feel established independently of the player.

Nora's Sunday routine contains the most important behavioral detail in the schedule.

She goes fishing alone.

She walks back along the river.

She approaches the stretch she avoids.

Then she turns around.

The player does not initially need an explanation.

It can simply appear to be a strange route choice.

Perhaps Nora always takes a longer path around that particular section.

Perhaps she stops briefly before changing direction.

Only later does the player learn why.

That makes her secret part of her actual life rather than information that suddenly becomes true when a heart event begins.

Severe rain provides another important opportunity.

Nora explicitly likes severe rain, which is unusual given her history.

That does not mean the Great Flood no longer affects her.

It can mean the opposite.

Storms give her something concrete to do.

She can tie down equipment.

Check the river.

Move nets.

Help people.

Tell jokes.

Stay busy.

During severe rain, Nora can actually become more energetic and talkative.

The behavior initially looks like resilience.

Once the player understands her better, it becomes clear that some of it is avoidance.

Her sea-shanty habit should also appear throughout her normal movement animations.

Nora does not need to stand still and perform an entire song.

She can hum or sing fragments while:

- Walking along the river
- Repairing nets
- Fishing
- Carrying equipment
- Checking the docks

That gives her an immediately recognizable auditory behavior in the world.

As Nora's character arc progresses, her schedule does not need to make her less optimistic.

Her optimism is genuine.

Her humor is genuine.

Her love for other people is genuine.

The growth is in allowing those qualities to coexist with sadness.

Early Nora reaches the avoided stretch of river and immediately turns around.

Later she may stand there for several minutes.

Eventually, a relationship event can finally take her there.

After completing her arc, the location can become part of her ordinary fishing rotation.

That would be a particularly strong piece of environmental character progression.

Nothing about the river changed.

Nora did.

Other schedule changes can remain similarly subtle.

Early Nora fills nearly every free evening with activity.

Later she may occasionally sit alone beside the river without fishing, singing, or talking to anyone.

Early Nora responds to a difficult day by becoming louder.

Later she may simply go home and tell Elise or the player that the day was hard.

She does not lose the laughter that makes her Nora.

She simply stops believing that everyone needs her to be laughing all the time.

Nora's routine therefore reinforces the central lesson of her character arc: weathering a storm does not mean pretending the rain never hurt. Sometimes resilience means allowing yourself to stand still long enough for someone else to stand beside you.
