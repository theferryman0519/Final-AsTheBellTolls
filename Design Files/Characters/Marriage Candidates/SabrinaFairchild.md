# Personality Information

Character Arc: Learning That Changing One Life Is Enough

Character Arc Start State: Sabrina believes a good teacher should be able to help every student succeed. Whenever someone struggles, she quietly blames herself, convinced she simply hasn't done enough.

Character Arc End State: Sabrina learns that she doesn't have to change everyone's life to make a difference. By celebrating the small victories and trusting her students to find their own paths, she discovers that compassion isn't measured by perfection.

Core Belief: Every person deserves someone who believes in them.

Core Misbelief: If someone I care about struggles, I must have failed them.

Core Traits: Compassion, Empathy, Idealism

Defining Sentence: ”Every child deserves someone who believes in them.”

Dialogue Length: Long

Emotional Need: To understand that offering kindness, guidance, and encouragement is enough—even when she can't solve every problem herself.

Favorite Location: In the library amongst the shelves of books

Fears: Sabrina fears watching someone give up on themselves because she wasn't able to reach them. She worries that she'll never become the teacher every child deserves.

Goals: To help every child in Blackmere discover their own strengths and grow into compassionate, confident adults.

Habit: Kneels to speak with children at eye level.

History: Sabrina always loved learning, but what inspired her most wasn't the lessons themselves—it was the teachers who believed in her when she doubted herself. After completing her education, she returned to Blackmere determined to become that source of encouragement for the next generation. Following the Great Flood, she worked tirelessly to restore a sense of normalcy for the town's children, believing that rebuilding hope was just as important as rebuilding homes.

Hobbies: Reading novels; collecting children's storybooks; pressing flowers into journals; watercolor painting; tending a small garden outside the school; writing encouraging notes for her students; birdwatching.

Motivation: Sabrina wants every child who walks through her classroom to leave believing they are capable of more than they ever imagined.

Personality Colors: Sage Green, Cream, Cornflower Blue

Personality Summary: Sabrina is one of Blackmere's kindest souls. Patient, empathetic, and endlessly encouraging, she has a remarkable ability to make people feel heard and valued. She naturally sees the potential in others long before they see it in themselves. However, her deep compassion often leads her to shoulder responsibility for problems she cannot fix, leaving her quietly questioning whether she's doing enough. Through her journey, she learns that genuine care isn't measured by how many people she saves, but by the lives she touches along the way.

Public Persona: Residents know Sabrina as the teacher who remembers every child's favorite book, notices when someone seems upset before they say a word, and always has time to listen. Her classroom is known as one of the warmest and safest places in Blackmere.

Secret: Sabrina keeps a small box filled with handwritten notes, drawings, and thank-you letters from her students. On days when she questions whether she's making a difference, she quietly rereads them to remind herself why she became a teacher.

Speaking Tone: Compassionate

Strength: Emotionally Intelligent

Weakness: Self-doubtful

# Schedule & Routine

## General Routine

Sabrina follows a warm, structured, and student-centered routine built around A. Whitmore Public School, the A. Whitmore Public Library, and the children of Blackmere.

As Blackmere's teacher, Sabrina spends Monday through Friday at A. Whitmore Public School.

The school is open from 8:00 AM to 3:00 PM. Sabrina generally arrives before opening to prepare lessons and remains briefly after classes end to organize the classroom, review student work, and prepare for the following day.

Sabrina lives at 3 Resident Lane with Adrian, her best friend.

The A. Whitmore Public Library is Sabrina's favorite location. She regularly visits after school to read, search for classroom materials, browse children's books, or simply spend quiet time among the shelves.

Because Adrian works at the library, Sabrina's visits also naturally provide opportunities for the two best friends to spend time together.

Sabrina maintains a small garden outside the school and tends it regularly when seasonal and weather conditions permit.

Her other hobbies include reading novels, collecting children's storybooks, pressing flowers into journals, watercolor painting, writing encouraging notes for students, and birdwatching.

Saturday and Sunday are Sabrina's days away from formal teaching.

Saturday is generally her more active personal day, while Sunday is quieter and focused on reading, gardening, creative hobbies, and preparing herself for the coming school week.

Sabrina's greatest difficulty is knowing when she has done enough. Even after the school day ends, she frequently continues thinking about lessons, students, and whether there is something more she could be doing to help.

## Weather Overrides

### Rainy

Sabrina generally follows her normal schedule during ordinary rainfall.

A. Whitmore Public School remains open.

Outdoor gardening and birdwatching activities are replaced with indoor activities.

Rainy free time usually includes:

- Reading
- Watercolor painting
- Pressing flowers
- Organizing classroom materials
- Writing encouraging notes
- Browsing the library when available

### Rainy (Severe)

A. Whitmore Public School and A. Whitmore Public Library are closed.

Sabrina dislikes severe rain and follows the Severe Weather Schedule.

Because the school is closed, she does not attempt to teach normal classes.

Sabrina remains at 3 Resident Lane and spends much of the day reading, preparing future lessons, painting, and worrying about whether the children are safe.

### Snowy

Sabrina generally follows her normal schedule.

A. Whitmore Public School remains open during ordinary snow.

Outdoor activities are shortened or replaced depending on conditions.

Sabrina may use snowy days for indoor reading activities with the students.

### Snowy (Severe)

A. Whitmore Public School and A. Whitmore Public Library are closed.

Sabrina follows the Severe Weather Schedule.

Although she is neutral toward severe snow personally, she worries about students and their families traveling in dangerous conditions and is relieved that the school closes.

### Windy

Sabrina generally follows her normal schedule.

Birdwatching and gardening activities may be shortened depending on conditions.

## Routine Characterization

Sabrina's routine is intentionally centered around care.

Unlike several other characters whose schedules are defined primarily by what they produce, Sabrina's work revolves around people.

The most important parts of her day are not simply "teaching."

She notices when Anya is unusually quiet.

She slows down when Felix does not understand something.

She encourages Kira's curiosity without letting it derail the entire lesson.

She finds ways to keep Milo engaged.

She kneels when speaking with children so she can meet them at eye level.

Those behaviors are what make Sabrina recognizable as Sabrina.

Her school schedule should therefore contain plenty of opportunities for contextual animations and NPC interactions rather than leaving her permanently standing at the front of a classroom.

She might:

- Write on a board
- Read beside a student
- Kneel beside a desk
- Carry books
- Water the garden
- Organize supplies
- Watch children during lunch
- Place encouraging notes on desks
- Read aloud to the class

Her favorite location being the library is equally important.

Sabrina genuinely loves learning and books independently of her profession.

However, her visits to the library can subtly reveal her central flaw.

She may enter intending to find one book for tomorrow's lesson.

Then she finds another.

Then another.

Soon she is researching an entirely new approach because one student struggled with something earlier that day.

Her compassion turns into self-imposed responsibility.

This is what makes Sabrina's overworking distinct from Clara, Mara, or Marcus.

Clara believes her work should always be better.

Mara believes she should be able to handle the work herself.

Marcus puts everyone else's needs ahead of his own.

Sabrina believes another person's struggle means she personally failed to help enough.

That distinction should drive her schedule.

The school garden also gives her an important activity outside the classroom.

It combines several elements of her personality:

- Patience
- Stewardship
- Teaching
- Flowers
- Nature
- Hope

The garden can potentially become something the children participate in rather than simply being Sabrina's private hobby.

That would fit her teaching style particularly well.

Adrian deserves a meaningful presence in Sabrina's routine because he is her best friend and housemate.

Their relationship should feel natural rather than scheduled solely for exposition.

They eat breakfast together.

They occasionally eat dinner together.

Sabrina visits Adrian's workplace because she genuinely loves the library.

They recommend books to one another.

They can spend evenings reading in the same room without needing constant conversation.

Their friendship exists whether or not the player interacts with either of them.

Friday evening gives Sabrina a broader social outlet.

With 85 Sociability and 100 Warmth, she should not spend every evening either at school or alone with books.

She genuinely enjoys people.

The distinction is that she tends to care for people even during social situations.

She may notice someone seems tired.

She may ask how Felix is doing.

She may remember something Jasmine mentioned last week.

Sabrina rarely stops emotionally monitoring the people around her.

Her Sunday routine is therefore especially important.

The small box of student notes and drawings already exists in her profile.

It should exist in her normal life as well.

Sabrina prepares for the coming week.

She reviews student progress.

She identifies someone who is struggling.

Her confidence begins to falter.

Then she opens the box.

Early in her arc, the purpose of the box is reassurance.

She needs evidence that she has helped people because she is afraid she has not done enough.

As her character arc develops, the meaning of that routine can change.

Perhaps she still opens the box.

But instead of looking for proof that she is a good teacher, she simply smiles at the memories.

Eventually, she may not need to open it every Sunday.

Her schedule can reflect her progression in other subtle ways.

Early Sabrina remains at school until 4:30 PM several days a week.

Later she might leave at 3:30 PM because the work can wait.

Early Sabrina spends Tuesday evening writing notes to every struggling student.

Later she writes one thoughtful note and then paints.

Early Sabrina enters the library searching desperately for another teaching strategy.

Later she sometimes chooses a novel simply because she wants to read it.

Early Sabrina reviews every student's progress on Sunday.

Later she recognizes their progress belongs partly to them.

These are small changes.

For Sabrina, they are enormous.

She does not need to become less compassionate.

She does not need to stop caring about her students.

She does not need to become a teacher who shrugs when someone struggles.

Her growth is learning that compassion does not grant her control over another person's life.

She can teach.

She can listen.

She can encourage.

She can believe in someone.

Sometimes that changes everything.

Sometimes it does not.

Neither outcome determines whether her kindness mattered.

Sabrina's routine therefore reinforces the central lesson of her character arc: changing one life is enough—and sometimes simply being the person who believed in someone was already more than enough.
