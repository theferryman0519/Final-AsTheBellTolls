# Personality Information

Character Arc: Learning That Choosing Isn't Losing

Character Arc Start State: Victor believes committing to one place, one opportunity, or one person means closing the door on countless others. He keeps his options open, convincing himself that freedom is worth more than permanence.

Character Arc End State: Victor learns that choosing where he belongs doesn't make his world smaller—it gives his adventures meaning. Commitment becomes something he chooses, not something that confines him.

Core Belief: Every road has something worth discovering.

Core Misbelief: If I commit to one path, I'll miss the one that was meant for me.

Core Traits: Charismatic, Curious, Guarded

Defining Sentence: ”Every stranger is simply a friend whose story I have yet to hear.”

Dialogue Length: Long

Emotional Need: To realize that belonging isn't the opposite of freedom. The right people can make every journey feel larger, not smaller.

Favorite Location: On the outskirts of town, where the road disappears over the horizon.

Fears: Victor fears becoming trapped in a predictable life where every day looks the same. More than anything, he worries about wondering "what if?" because he stopped exploring too soon.

Goals: To discover rare goods, remarkable stories, and hidden places while helping Blackmere become a destination that travelers remember long after they've moved on.

Habit: Twirls a trading coin between his fingers during bartering.

History: Victor spent much of his life traveling alongside merchant caravans, learning that every town has its own stories, traditions, and treasures. Rather than inheriting a permanent shop, he embraced life on the road, becoming as skilled at reading people as he was at finding valuable trade opportunities. After the Great Flood, he chose to make Blackmere one of his regular stops, recognizing both its need for supplies and the resilience of its people. Over time, the temporary stop became harder to leave than he ever expected.

Hobbies: Collecting unusual trinkets from distant regions; journaling about places he's visited; playing card games with fellow travelers; learning local customs wherever he goes; birdwatching during long journeys; stargazing beside his caravan.

Motivation: Victor wants to connect people through trade, believing every exchanged item carries a story from somewhere else in the world.

Personality Colors: Teal, Amber, Burgundy

Personality Summary: Victor is charming, endlessly curious, and effortlessly adaptable. He thrives on meeting new people, discovering unfamiliar places, and uncovering stories hidden in everyday objects. His warmth and humor make him easy to like, but beneath his outgoing personality is someone reluctant to become tied down. Accustomed to leaving before attachments grow too deep, Victor struggles to believe that commitment can be a choice rather than a limitation. His journey teaches him that finding a place to belong doesn't mean giving up the excitement of discovery—it gives him someone to share it with.

Public Persona: Most residents know Victor as the charismatic trader who always has an interesting story, an unusual item for sale, or a clever bargain to offer. He seems to know everyone, remembers every conversation, and has a knack for making strangers feel like old friends.

Secret: Victor still keeps a packed travel bag tucked beneath his bed, ready to leave at a moment's notice. He tells himself it's simply good preparation, but the truth is he isn't sure he's ever allowed himself to truly unpack anywhere.

Speaking Tone: Guarded

Strength: Adaptability

Weakness: Non-committal

# Schedule & Routine

## General Routine

Victor follows a flexible, socially active, and travel-oriented routine centered around the Caravan Stall, the roads leading into and out of Blackmere, and places where travelers and residents naturally gather.

As Blackmere's trader, Victor spends most working days selling imported goods, negotiating prices, organizing trade stock, speaking with travelers, hearing stories from other regions, and keeping track of opportunities beyond Blackmere.

The Caravan Stall is open from 10:00 AM to 6:00 PM Monday through Saturday.

Victor does not begin his mornings at the stall. He prefers to spend the early part of the day walking, checking the road, speaking with arriving travelers, organizing trade goods, or simply enjoying the feeling of being somewhere between staying and leaving.

Sunday is the Caravan Stall's weekly closed day.

Victor treats Sunday as his most personal and unpredictable day. He spends more time around Blackmere's outskirts, visiting friends, journaling about places he has traveled, playing cards, or watching the road.

Victor lives at 5 Resident Lane with Dante and Madison.

Dante is Victor's best friend, so their shared home and regular conversations are an important part of Victor's routine.

Victor also considers Roland a mentor. Roland's influence gives Victor one of the few relationships in which someone older and more settled can challenge his assumption that permanence automatically means limitation.

Victor's greatest difficulty is allowing any routine to feel permanent.

Even after choosing Blackmere as a regular stop, he continues behaving as though he may leave again at any time.

Outdoor morning walks and evening trips to the outskirts are shortened or replaced with indoor social activities.

- Winding Banks Inn

The Caravan Stall is closed.

Victor follows the Severe Weather Schedule instead of his normal routine.

Victor dislikes severe rain and avoids unnecessary travel through Blackmere.

He remains primarily at 5 Resident Lane or Winding Banks Inn when safe and available.

### Snowy

Victor generally follows his normal schedule.

He shortens long walks along the road but remains interested in travelers arriving from other regions and how weather has affected their journeys.

### Snowy (Severe)

The Caravan Stall is closed.

Victor likes windy weather and generally follows his normal schedule.

He may remain at the road longer than usual, watching clouds, birds, and travelers moving across the horizon.

## Trade Journey Override

Victor periodically leaves Blackmere on short trading journeys.

These journeys are an important part of his profession and prevent the Caravan Stall from feeling as though its imported goods appear without explanation.

Trade journeys temporarily replace Victor's normal schedule for the duration of the trip.

Possible purposes include:

- Purchasing unusual goods
- Meeting traveling merchants
- Trading Blackmere products for regional items
- Gathering information about markets and roads
- Learning about local customs in neighboring communities
- Finding rare goods requested by residents

Victor should not leave Blackmere every week.

Trade journeys should occur occasionally enough that his absence feels notable.

When Victor is away:

- The Caravan Stall may be closed or unavailable depending on the final shop implementation
- Victor is unavailable for normal interactions
- His return may introduce different stock
- New dialogue may reference where he traveled
- He may bring back unusual stories or objects

Trade journeys are one of the clearest expressions of Victor's central conflict.

At the beginning of his arc, leaving feels natural and returning feels temporary.

As his connection to Blackmere deepens, returning gradually becomes the part of the journey that matters most.

## Routine Characterization

Victor's routine is intentionally less predictable than those of most other professional residents.

The Caravan Stall gives his week a clear anchor, but Victor himself should never feel entirely anchored.

He begins many mornings away from the stall.

He walks the road.

He watches travelers.

He talks to strangers.

He checks what is happening beyond the center of town.

That behavior matters because Victor spent much of his life traveling.

Blackmere is not simply the place where his workplace happens to be.

It is the place he has unexpectedly continued choosing to return to.

His professional routine should also feel distinctly different from a conventional shopkeeper.

Victor does not simply stand behind a counter and sell fixed inventory.

He bargains.

He trades.

He remembers what customers wanted last week.

He tells stories about where an object came from.

He hears rumors about other towns.

He learns which goods are becoming harder to find.

He watches travelers arrive carrying things Blackmere has never seen before.

Every object is connected to somewhere else.

That is what Victor loves about trade.

His extraordinarily high sociability is also reflected throughout his schedule.

Victor enjoys strangers.

He enjoys conversation.

He enjoys entering a room where he knows nobody and leaving with three new stories.

Winding Banks Inn therefore becomes a particularly natural location for him.

Travelers stay there.

Stories pass through there.

Card games happen there.

For Victor, an evening at the inn is almost a miniature version of being back on the road.

Dante's presence in Victor's routine provides an important contrast.

Dante preserves objects because of where they have been.

Victor trades objects because of where they might go next.

Both men care deeply about the stories attached to things, which gives their friendship a strong foundation despite their very different personalities.

Victor can bring Dante some strange trinket from another region.

Dante may spend twenty minutes explaining its historical significance.

Victor probably interrupts halfway through with a better story about the person who sold it to him.

Their friendship should feel alive independently of the player.

Roland fills a different role.

As Victor's mentor, Roland represents someone with enough life experience to challenge Victor's assumption that choosing a place means surrendering possibility.

Their Sunday visits should not necessarily involve direct emotional conversations every week.

Sometimes they may simply talk.

The consistency of Victor choosing to visit Roland is itself meaningful.

Victor's favorite location is one of the most important pieces of his schedule.

He likes standing on the outskirts of Blackmere where the road disappears over the horizon.

At that exact location, Victor can look in two directions.

One direction leads away.

The other leads home.

Early in his story, his body language can subtly favor the road outward.

He watches departing travelers.

He studies maps.

He flips his trading coin.

He looks as though he may begin walking at any moment.

That image visually communicates his central conflict without requiring dialogue.

Sunday makes that conflict especially visible.

The Caravan Stall is closed.

Nothing professionally requires him to remain in Blackmere.

So he goes to the road.

And then he comes back.

His packed travel bag serves the same purpose inside 5 Resident Lane.

Victor's profile explicitly establishes that he keeps it beneath his bed.

The bag should therefore be part of his normal routine rather than appearing only during a relationship event.

Every Sunday evening, Victor checks it.

He rearranges something.

He makes sure everything is still ready.

But he does not leave.

At the beginning of his arc, the bag represents an escape route.

Victor can tell himself that nothing has really changed because he could still disappear tomorrow.

As his character develops, the routine can subtly evolve.

He checks the bag less often.

Something gets removed.

Then something else.

Eventually, the bag may still exist, because Victor remains a traveler.

The difference is that it becomes luggage rather than an emergency exit.

His trade journeys should reinforce the same progression.

Victor should continue leaving Blackmere occasionally even after completing his arc.

His lesson is not that travel was wrong.

Travel is one of the things he loves most.

The growth is in understanding the difference between traveling from somewhere and running from belonging.

Early Victor leaves and thinks primarily about what he might discover.

Later Victor leaves and also thinks about what he will bring back.

After marriage, that distinction becomes even stronger.

Pendrelle Manor should never become a cage for him.

If marriage required Victor to stop traveling, it would prove his original fear correct.

Instead, he travels and returns.

The return is the choice.

His schedule can show his progression in other small ways.

Early Victor spends most free evenings at Winding Banks Inn or the outskirts.

Later he sometimes stays home with Dante and Madison.

Early Victor keeps every possession portable.

Later more trinkets appear around his room.

Early Victor describes Blackmere as one stop among many.

Later he unconsciously says "home."

None of these changes make his world smaller.

They give all of those distant roads somewhere to lead back to.

Victor's routine therefore reinforces the central lesson of his character arc: choosing one place does not close every other road. Sometimes choosing where you belong is what finally makes every journey mean something.
