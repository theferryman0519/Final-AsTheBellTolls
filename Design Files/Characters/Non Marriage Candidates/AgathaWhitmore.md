# Personality Information

Character Arc: Learning That a Legacy Lives Through Change

Character Arc Start State: Agatha believes preserving Blackmere's traditions is the best way to honor the sacrifices made by its founders. She often resists new ideas, fearing they'll slowly erase the town she helped build.

Character Arc End State: Agatha comes to understand that Blackmere's legacy isn't found in preserving every tradition unchanged, but in passing its values to a new generation willing to shape its own future.

Core Belief: A community is built one generation at a time.

Core Misbelief: If we let go of our traditions, we'll eventually forget who we are.

Core Traits: Blunt, Insightful, Learned

Defining Sentence: ”Wisdom is earned by listening longer than you speak.”

Dialogue Length: Mid

Emotional Need: To realize that change doesn't erase a legacy—it allows it to continue through the people who come after us.

Favorite Location: Amongst her namesake library and school

Fears: Agatha fears that the stories, sacrifices, and values that founded Blackmere will be forgotten as newer generations focus only on the future. More personally, she worries that one day no one will remember the people who built the town from nothing.

Goals: To ensure Blackmere's history, traditions, and values are preserved while preparing its next generation to lead with wisdom and compassion.

Habit: Takes a thoughtful sip of tea before engaging in conversation.

History: Decades ago, Agatha and her husband, Roland, were among the first families to settle what would become Blackmere. Together with a handful of other pioneers, they helped transform an untamed settlement into a thriving community through determination, cooperation, and countless years of hard work. Agatha believed that a prosperous town required more than sturdy buildings—it needed educated minds and a shared sense of purpose. She became one of Blackmere's earliest advocates for public education and access to knowledge, helping establish the town's first schoolhouse and library. Over time, both institutions grew into cornerstones of the community and were eventually renamed in recognition of her lifelong dedication. Though the Great Flood changed much of the town she helped build, Agatha remains determined to ensure that Blackmere's history is remembered as its future is rebuilt.

Hobbies: Reading historical journals; preserving family records; tending a perennial garden; quilting; mentoring younger residents; organizing historical archives; writing memoirs about Blackmere's early years.

Motivation: Agatha wants every generation of Blackmere to understand that the town's greatest strength has always been its people, not its buildings.

Personality Colors: Burgundy, Forest Green, Gold

Personality Summary: Agatha is one of Blackmere's oldest and most respected residents, though few would describe her as gentle. Intelligent, observant, and exceptionally well-read, she has little patience for excuses, half-measures, or empty pleasantries. She says exactly what she's thinking, regardless of who might be standing in front of her, believing that honesty is ultimately kinder than comfortable lies. Despite her sharp tongue, Agatha deeply cares about Blackmere and its people. She simply expresses that care through hard truths, practical wisdom, and unwavering expectations rather than comforting words. Those who know her well understand that if Agatha takes the time to criticize you, it's because she believes you're capable of better.

Public Persona: Most residents consider Agatha one of Blackmere's wisest—and most intimidating—voices. Asking for her opinion means accepting that you'll receive it unfiltered. She has little interest in sparing egos and even less interest in flattery, earning a reputation for delivering brutally honest advice that often proves correct. While some avoid her sharp remarks, many quietly seek her guidance because they know Agatha will never tell them what they want to hear—only what she believes they need to hear.

Secret: Agatha quietly worries that she has become more attached to preserving Blackmere's past than helping shape its future. She keeps journals documenting the town's earliest days, not because she's afraid of forgetting, but because she's afraid no one else will remember once she's gone.

Speaking Tone: Dry

Strength: Traditional

Weakness: Resisting

# Schedule & Routine

## General Routine

Agatha follows a deliberate and traditional routine centered around 1 Resident Lane, the A. Whitmore Public Library, the A. Whitmore Public School, and Blackmere's community.

Agatha has no formal workplace and considers herself well past the age when anyone should be assigning her working hours.

That does not mean she is inactive.

As one of Blackmere's earliest settlers and most respected elders, Agatha spends much of her time preserving historical records, reading, writing about Blackmere's early years, tending her perennial garden, mentoring younger residents, and observing how the town is changing.

Agatha lives at 1 Resident Lane with her husband Roland and their daughter Beatrice.

The A. Whitmore Public Library and A. Whitmore Public School are Agatha's favorite locations.

She was instrumental in establishing both institutions, and their eventual naming in her honor makes them deeply personal to her.

Agatha regularly visits the library to read historical journals, organize archival material, speak with Adrian, and observe how Blackmere's written history is being preserved.

She also periodically visits the school to speak with Sabrina, observe the younger generation, assist with historical lessons, and remind the children that Blackmere existed before they did.

Agatha maintains a perennial garden at 1 Resident Lane and tends it regularly when seasonal and weather conditions permit.

Her hobbies also include quilting, preserving family records, mentoring younger residents, organizing historical archives, and writing memoirs about Blackmere's earliest years.

Agatha values routine and does not change her plans without reason.

Her greatest difficulty is accepting that preserving Blackmere's legacy does not require preserving Blackmere exactly as she remembers it.

## Weather Overrides

### Rainy

Agatha likes rainy weather and generally follows her normal schedule.

She enjoys sitting near a window with tea while reading, quilting, or working on historical records.

If she has no scheduled reason to leave home, she becomes somewhat more likely to remain at 1 Resident Lane.

Garden work is replaced with:

- Reading historical journals
- Quilting
- Organizing family records
- Writing memoirs
- Drinking tea with Roland

The A. Whitmore Public School and A. Whitmore Public Library remain open during ordinary rain.

### Rainy (Severe)

The A. Whitmore Public School and A. Whitmore Public Library are closed.

Agatha follows the Severe Weather Schedule.

She remains primarily at 1 Resident Lane.

Unlike many residents, Agatha is not particularly frightened by severe rain.

She has lived through enough difficult weather to treat preparation and patience as more useful than panic.

However, severe rain inevitably reminds her of the Great Flood and the Blackmere that existed before it.

### Snowy

Agatha likes snowy weather and generally follows her normal schedule.

She limits unnecessary walking but enjoys watching snowfall from 1 Resident Lane or the library.

Garden work is replaced with indoor hobbies.

### Snowy (Severe)

The A. Whitmore Public School and A. Whitmore Public Library are closed.

Agatha follows the Severe Weather Schedule.

She remains at 1 Resident Lane and spends the day reading, quilting, writing, and organizing records.

### Windy

Agatha dislikes windy weather.

She generally follows essential portions of her normal schedule but avoids unnecessary outdoor walks.

Garden work and leisurely walks through Blackmere are replaced with indoor activities.

She is noticeably irritated by anything the wind knocks over, scatters, or otherwise puts out of order.

## Town Restoration Routine Override

Agatha pays close attention to whether Blackmere's recovery preserves its history while still allowing the town to change.

### Weathered / Rebuilding

Agatha visits damaged civic and historical locations more frequently, particularly the library, school, Town Hall, and longstanding community landmarks.

### Recovering / Renewed / Growing

She revisits restored spaces to judge how well their history has been preserved and increasingly uses the library, school, and public areas for conversations with younger residents.

### Prospering / Flourishing

Agatha spends less time inspecting restoration and more time using the spaces themselves. Her routine increasingly reflects mentorship, reading, conversation, and participation in the Blackmere that followed the flood.

## Routine Characterization

Agatha's routine is intentionally slower and more deliberate than those of Blackmere's younger residents.

She does not have a profession requiring her to be somewhere for eight hours.

She does not need to rush from one activity to another.

She has earned the right to decide what deserves her time.

That does not make her passive.

Agatha remains deeply involved in Blackmere.

She simply participates differently.

Her influence comes through:

- Memory
- Advice
- Education
- Historical preservation
- Family
- Expectations
- Observation

The library and school are therefore essential to her schedule.

They are not merely buildings carrying Agatha's surname.

She helped create the institutions that eventually became them.

When Agatha walks through the library, she is walking through an idea she helped establish decades earlier: that Blackmere's residents deserved access to knowledge.

When she enters the school, she is seeing generations of children benefit from something that began when Blackmere itself was young.

That should create complicated emotions.

Agatha is proud of those institutions.

She is also protective of them.

Perhaps excessively so.

Her visits should sometimes make Adrian or Sabrina slightly nervous.

Not because Agatha is cruel.

Because if she notices something, she will say something.

A crooked shelf.

A poorly organized archive.

A historical date written incorrectly.

A lesson she thinks underestimates the children.

Agatha does not believe age requires everyone else to agree with her.

She does, however, believe experience deserves to be taken seriously.

That makes Sabrina particularly interesting for her.

Sabrina represents the current generation responsible for the institution Agatha helped establish.

Agatha may respect Sabrina's compassion while occasionally questioning newer teaching approaches.

Their conversations can provide small, everyday demonstrations of Agatha's character arc.

The same applies to Adrian at the library.

Agatha may know how the library was intended to function.

Adrian knows how the library needs to function now.

Neither perspective is inherently wrong.

Agatha's challenge is recognizing that an institution can change without betraying the reason it was created.

Her home routine should be equally important.

Agatha lives with Roland and Beatrice.

These are not simply three unrelated NPC schedules occupying the same building.

They are a family.

Roland and Agatha have spent decades together.

Their relationship should therefore contain comfortable silence.

They do not need elaborate date nights every week.

They can drink tea.

Read in the same room.

Walk through Blackmere.

Argue mildly about some detail from forty years ago.

Continue drinking tea.

That kind of routine can communicate a long marriage more effectively than overtly romantic behavior.

Beatrice adds another layer.

Agatha is not merely one of the mayor's constituents.

She is the mayor's mother.

Friday's Town Hall visit therefore carries both civic and familial meaning.

Agatha can offer opinions nobody else would dare give Beatrice quite so directly.

That relationship also gives Agatha a literal connection between Blackmere's founding generation and its current leadership.

Her garden provides a quieter expression of the same theme.

Agatha tends perennial plants.

That choice is particularly appropriate for her.

Perennials return.

They survive seasons.

They grow from roots established years earlier.

But they are not identical every year.

That is almost Agatha's entire character arc expressed through gardening.

Her historical journals serve a similar purpose.

Agatha's secret establishes that she is not documenting Blackmere because she fears that she herself will forget.

She is afraid everyone else will.

Sunday evening makes that fear part of her ordinary routine.

She opens an old journal.

She reads about people who are gone.

Then she adds another entry.

Early in her arc, those entries may focus heavily on preserving facts exactly as they were.

Who built something.

When something happened.

How a tradition was performed.

What Blackmere used to look like.

As Agatha develops, the content can subtly shift.

She begins writing about what is happening now.

A restored building.

Something one of the children said.

A new invention.

A decision Beatrice made differently than Agatha would have.

Something the player changed.

Her journal gradually stops being only a record of the Blackmere that was.

It becomes a record of the Blackmere that is becoming.

That would be an excellent environmental representation of her character progression.

Her schedule can change subtly as well.

Early Agatha visits the school partly to ensure things are being done properly.

Later she visits because she genuinely enjoys seeing what Sabrina and the children are creating.

Early Agatha corrects Adrian's archival choices.

Later she asks why he chose them.

Early Agatha walks through restored Blackmere comparing everything to what stood there before.

Later she begins evaluating new places on their own merits.

She never becomes progressive simply for the sake of being progressive.

Her Tradition score does not need to disappear.

Agatha should remain the person who remembers why Blackmere does things the way it does.

That is valuable.

Her growth is recognizing that tradition is strongest when its values survive adaptation—not when every detail is frozen permanently.

Agatha's routine therefore reinforces the central lesson of her character arc: a legacy is not something preserved behind glass. It survives when the next generation understands what mattered, carries it forward, and has the courage to build something of its own.
