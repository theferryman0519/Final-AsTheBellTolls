# Personality Information

Character Arc: Learning That Leadership Means Moving Forward

Character Arc Start State: Beatrice believes every loss Blackmere suffered during the Great Flood is ultimately her responsibility. She carries the weight of every decision she made, convinced that a better leader could have prevented more tragedy.

Character Arc End State: Beatrice learns that leadership isn't measured by never making mistakes—it's measured by giving people hope, even after unimaginable loss. By forgiving herself, she allows Blackmere to move forward alongside her.

Core Belief: A leader exists to serve the people before themselves.

Core Misbelief: If I had been a better mayor, more people would still be alive.

Core Traits: Authoritative, Dependable, Guarded

Defining Sentence: ”A community grows strongest when everyone carries a little of the weight.”

Dialogue Length: Mid

Emotional Need: To accept that she cannot change the past, and that honoring those who were lost means helping the living build a better future.

Favorite Location: Within Town Square amongst the crowds and buildings

Fears: Beatrice fears failing the people who have placed their trust in her. More than criticism or political failure, she fears making another decision that costs innocent lives.

Goals: To restore Blackmere into a thriving community where every resident has the opportunity to rebuild not only their home, but also their future.

Habit: Straightens a stack of paperwork while she thinks.

History: Born to Agatha and Roland Whitmore, two of Blackmere's founding families, Beatrice was raised with a deep sense of duty to her community. Although she never aspired to become mayor, she accepted the role because she believed Blackmere needed someone willing to put its people first. The Great Flood forever changed both the town and her, and despite leading rescue and recovery efforts, she continues to quietly blame herself for every life that was lost. Ever since, she has dedicated herself to rebuilding Blackmere, determined to create a future worthy of those the town lost.

Hobbies: Walking through Town Square before sunrise; tending the flower beds surrounding Town Hall; reading civic history and biographies; organizing community events; writing letters to neighboring towns; quietly enjoying tea with her parents.

Motivation: Beatrice wants every resident of Blackmere to believe that tomorrow can be better than yesterday, no matter what they have lost.

Personality Colors: Royal Blue, Ivory, Burgundy

Personality Summary: Beatrice is a composed and dependable leader who places the needs of Blackmere above her own. Calm under pressure and unwavering in her sense of duty, she rarely allows others to see the emotional burden she carries. While she projects confidence and authority, much of her determination is fueled by lingering guilt over the lives lost during the Great Flood. She believes leadership is a responsibility to be earned every day, often holding herself to standards no one else expects of her. Through her journey, she learns that a leader's greatest strength isn't perfection—it's the courage to keep guiding others forward despite their own regrets.

Public Persona: Most residents see Beatrice as the steady heart of Blackmere's recovery. Professional, organized, and unwaveringly dependable, she is often the first to arrive during a crisis and the last to leave once the work is finished. Though some mistake her reserved demeanor for emotional distance, those who know her understand that every difficult decision she makes comes from a genuine desire to protect the people of Blackmere.

Secret: Every year on the anniversary of the Great Flood, Beatrice quietly visits Memorial Hill before dawn with a fresh bouquet of dahlias. She reads through the names of those who were lost and privately apologizes to each of them, believing she should have done more to save them.

Speaking Tone: Guarded

Strength: Leadership

Weakness: Distrusting

# Schedule & Routine

## General Routine

Beatrice follows a highly structured routine centered around Blackmere Town Hall, Town Square, and the ongoing restoration of Blackmere.

As Blackmere's mayor, Beatrice spends most days managing town affairs, reviewing restoration efforts, meeting with residents, answering correspondence, organizing community projects, and making decisions regarding Blackmere's recovery.

Blackmere Town Hall is open from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM every day.

Beatrice generally arrives before opening and remains until closing.

Unlike most residents with formal workplaces, Beatrice does not maintain a regular weekly day off. She considers the responsibilities of mayor to exist regardless of the day of the week.

However, her weekends are somewhat lighter than her weekday schedule and contain more time with her parents, walking through Blackmere, reading, and tending the flower beds surrounding Town Hall.

Beatrice lives at 1 Resident Lane with her parents, Agatha and Roland.

She regularly shares breakfast, dinner, or evening tea with them when her responsibilities allow.

Town Square is Beatrice's favorite location.

She frequently walks through the district before or after Town Hall hours to observe restoration progress, inspect public spaces, speak with residents, and remain connected to the community she governs.

Madison is Beatrice's best friend. Their relationship combines genuine friendship with frequent professional interaction because Madison's responsibilities as sheriff overlap with Beatrice's responsibilities as mayor.

Beatrice also considers Lena a mentor and occasionally seeks her perspective when difficult decisions involve Blackmere's residents or its recovery.

Beatrice's greatest difficulty is knowing when responsibility ends.

She frequently stays occupied because stopping gives her time to reconsider decisions she made during and after the Great Flood.

## Weather Overrides

### Rainy

Beatrice generally follows her normal schedule.

Blackmere Town Hall remains open during ordinary rain.

Morning and evening walks through Town Square are shortened.

Flower-bed maintenance is replaced with:

- Reviewing correspondence
- Civic planning
- Reading
- Organizing community events
- Additional administrative work

### Rainy (Severe)

Beatrice dislikes severe rain.

Blackmere Town Hall remains open and has no weather closure.

Beatrice follows the Severe Weather Schedule.

Severe rain places Beatrice into an emergency-management mindset because of her experiences during the Great Flood.

She becomes more focused on:

- Public safety
- Weather conditions
- Emergency preparation
- Vulnerable residents
- River conditions
- Ensuring essential services remain available

She avoids unnecessary travel but remains at Town Hall throughout its operating hours.

### Snowy

Beatrice generally follows her normal schedule.

Outdoor inspections and flower-bed work are shortened when necessary.

### Snowy (Severe)

Beatrice dislikes severe snow.

Blackmere Town Hall remains open.

Beatrice follows the Severe Weather Schedule and prioritizes public safety, road conditions, emergency planning, and resident welfare.

### Windy

Beatrice generally follows her normal schedule.

Flower-bed maintenance may be shortened or replaced if conditions make it impractical.

## Town Inspection Override

As Blackmere's restoration progresses, Beatrice occasionally replaces part of an afternoon at Town Hall with an inspection of an active restoration project.

Possible destinations depend on the current restoration state and active projects.

These inspections may involve:

- Speaking with the resident responsible for the project
- Reviewing safety or accessibility
- Checking whether public resources are being used appropriately
- Observing construction progress
- Speaking with affected residents
- Recording additional work that may be required

Beatrice does not perform construction herself.

Her purpose is to understand the condition of the town she is responsible for governing.

Town inspections become more frequent during major restoration milestones.

## Routine Characterization

Beatrice's routine is intentionally one of the most structured in Blackmere.

Her job does not simply occupy her working hours. She considers herself responsible for the town from the moment she wakes until the moment she goes to sleep.

Her morning walks through Town Square are important because they keep her connected to Blackmere outside reports and paperwork. She wants to see whether streets are improving, whether residents are returning, and whether the town feels alive again.

Her friendship with Madison also naturally overlaps with her work. Beatrice trusts Madison more than almost anyone, making their professional meetings one of the few places where Beatrice can occasionally lower her guard.

The danger is that Beatrice uses responsibility to avoid confronting her guilt. There is always another report to read, another resident to help, or another restoration project to inspect.

Her progression should therefore involve small changes rather than abandoning her work.

Early Beatrice takes paperwork home almost every night.

Later she occasionally leaves it at Town Hall.

Early Beatrice believes every setback reflects a failure of leadership.

Later she allows other residents to carry some of the responsibility.

Early Beatrice walks through Town Square looking primarily for problems.

Later she begins noticing what Blackmere has already rebuilt.

Her routine reinforces the lesson at the center of her character arc: leadership does not mean carrying the entire town alone. It means helping the community move forward together.
