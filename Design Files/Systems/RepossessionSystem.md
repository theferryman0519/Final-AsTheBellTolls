## Information

- The player learns during the introduction that Blackmere may eventually be repossessed by the government if the town cannot demonstrate that it has become viable.
- The player receives two full in-game years before the first formal government assessment.
- On Day 1 of Spring, Year 3, Regent Rupert returns to Blackmere for the Year 3 Assessment.
    - Rupert evaluates Blackmere through five assessment pillars rather than through a single restoration-completion percentage.
    - The assessment has a maximum score of 200 points.
    - Each pillar contributes a maximum of 40 points.
    - More than 40 points of valid evidence may exist inside a pillar, but the pillar score is capped at 40. This allows different saves to demonstrate viability in different ways.
    - No single pillar must be maximized in order to pass.
    - The player can review the five pillar scores and all score-producing requirements before the assessment. The assessment is not a hidden perfection check.
- Rupert's assessment is presented as a physical inspection of Blackmere.
    - Rupert tours approximately four to six locations based on the actual state of the save.
    - Strong and weak pillars influence which locations are selected and what Rupert comments on.
    - The tour is presentation of the assessment evidence; it does not secretly add or remove points.
- A Year 3 pass resolves the immediate repossession threat.
- A Year 3 non-pass becomes the One Last Chance conditional state.
    - Rupert issues two or three targeted covenants based on the weakest pillars in that save.
    - The player can review covenant requirements and progress throughout Year 3.
    - Rupert returns on Day 1 of Spring, Year 4 only when a Year 3 conditional result was issued.
- Failing the Year 4 reassessment does not delete the save, remove restoration, reset relationships, or end the campaign.
    - Blackmere enters a restrictive continuation state under Regency oversight.
    - The player can continue improving Blackmere and eventually regain full autonomy.

## Assessment Pillars

| Pillar | Purpose | Maximum Score |
|---|---|---:|
| Structural Stability | Essential buildings, public access, safe routes, dependable infrastructure, and reliable physical services | 40 |
| Economic Stability | Operating businesses, trade, treasury health, debt management, production, and sustainable economic activity | 40 |
| Civic Continuity | Town Hall, education, records, public services, public institutions, and community activities | 40 |
| Community Stability | Resident relationships, household recovery, dependable routines, communal participation, NPC-to-NPC development, and social support | 40 |
| Future Readiness | Inventions, cross-location synergies, regional connections, long-term resilience, and evidence that Blackmere can continue adapting | 40 |
| TOTAL |  | 200 |

### Pillar Scoring Rule

- Each pillar contains more valid scoring opportunities than are required to reach its 40-point cap.
- Once a pillar reaches 40 points, additional evidence remains visible but does not increase the assessment total.
- Scoring requirements are additive unless a row specifically states that only the highest completed tier is counted.
- Relationship scoring never requires romance, marriage, or children.
- Restoration scoring uses the normal seven restoration states:
    - Weathered = 0
    - Rebuilding = 1
    - Recovering = 2
    - Renewed = 3
    - Growing = 4
    - Prospering = 5
    - Flourishing = 6

## Assessment Breakdown

### Structural Stability

| Action | Allotted Points |
|---|---:|
| Essential Blackmere location reaches at least Recovering | 4 points per qualifying location, max of 16 points |
| An essential Blackmere location already counted above reaches at least Growing | +2 points per qualifying location, max of 12 additional points |
| A town district has dependable public access through repaired or stabilized routes, bridges, crossings, or equivalent access | 2 points per district, max of 8 points |
| Complete a structural, transport, utility, flood-resilience, or other infrastructure improvement recognized by the Repossession System | 2 points per improvement, max of 8 points |
| PILLAR CAP | 40 points |

Structural Evidence Notes

- A location may qualify for both the Recovering and Growing rows.
- The eligible essential-location list is defined in assessment data so the player can see exactly which locations count.
- Restoration does not need to follow one specific order. Any qualifying essential locations can provide the required evidence.

---

### Economic Stability

| Action | Allotted Points |
|---|---:|
| A Blackmere business is restored enough to provide its dependable core service | 2 points per operating business, max of 12 points |
| An operating business reaches at least Growing and provides expanded stock, service, production, or town contribution | +2 points per qualifying business, max of 10 additional points |
| Establish an active regional trade relationship or dependable visiting-trader connection | 4 points per relationship, max of 12 points |
| Town treasury is currently stable rather than declining | 4 points |
| Town treasury maintains a positive reserve | 7 points instead of 4 |
| Town treasury maintains a sustained positive reserve/trend | 10 points instead of 4 or 7 |
| Player has no overdue loan obligations | 3 points |
| Player has fully repaid all government/bank loans that count toward the assessment | 6 points instead of 3 |
| Maintain meaningful production or sales activity across distinct economic categories | 2 points per qualifying category, max of 8 points |
| PILLAR CAP | 40 points |

Economic Evidence Notes

- Treasury rows are tiered; only the highest currently satisfied treasury row is counted.
- Loan rows are tiered; only the highest currently satisfied loan row is counted.
- Exact treasury-health thresholds are owned by the Economy System and must be visible to the player before they affect assessment scoring.
- The assessment does not require the player to become debt-free if enough other economic evidence demonstrates stability.

| Action | Allotted Points |
|---|---:|
| Town Hall restoration state | 0-6 points using the restoration-state value |
| A. Whitmore Public School restoration state | 0-6 points using the restoration-state value |
| A. Whitmore Public Library restoration state | 0-6 points using the restoration-state value |
| Hall of Wonder restoration state | 0-6 points using the restoration-state value |
| Restore or maintain a dependable civic/public service recognized by the Repossession System | 2 points per service, max of 8 points |
| Support recurring public activities such as school, library, museum, civic, festival, or community programming | 2 points per qualifying activity type, max of 8 points |
| Complete major records, Archive of Echoes, library, museum, or public-history milestones | 2 points per milestone, max of 8 points |
| PILLAR CAP | 40 points |

Civic Evidence Notes

- Full Library or Hall of Wonder completion is not required for Year 3 viability.
- Collection and archive progress can strengthen the pillar without becoming a mandatory completionist gate.
- Civic scoring rewards institutions being used, not only visually restored.

### Community Stability

| Action | Allotted Points |
|---|---:|
| Resident reaches at least 5 Hearts with the player | 1 point per resident, max of 12 points |
| A resident already counted above reaches at least 8 Hearts | +1 point per resident, max of 8 additional points |
| Complete a resident Connection / Bonding milestone | 2 points per qualifying milestone, max of 10 points |
| Complete or advance a recognized NPC-to-NPC relationship-chain milestone | 2 points per milestone, max of 8 points |
| Complete a communal participation or reciprocal-assistance milestone | 2 points per milestone, max of 10 points |
| Restore an occupied residential/household location enough for dependable daily use | 2 points per household, max of 8 points |
| PILLAR CAP | 40 points |

Community Evidence Notes

- No romance-specific state is required.
- Marriage and children do not award unique assessment points that cannot be earned by a single or child-free player.
- NPC-to-NPC progression counts because Rupert is evaluating whether Blackmere functions as a community, not only whether residents like the player.
- Relationship breadth, deeper bonds, household recovery, and communal participation provide interchangeable routes toward the pillar cap.

### Future Readiness

| Action | Allotted Points |
|---|---:|
| Complete a major Blueprint Book branch/tier milestone | 4 points per milestone, max of 16 points |
| Install a Civic / Installed Invention that produces an active world or service effect | 3 points per invention, max of 12 points |
| Activate a cross-location restoration synergy | 3 points per active synergy, max of 12 points |

Future Readiness Notes

- Grand Showcase rows are tiered; only the highest completed result is counted.
- Winning the Grand Showcase is not required to pass the assessment.
- Blueprint progress alone cannot fill the entire pillar.
- Installed inventions and synergies must be active in the world rather than merely crafted and stored.

## Year 3 Assessment

### Full Pass

The town receives a Full Pass when all of the following are true:

```text
TotalAssessmentScore >= 140
AND PillarsAtOrAbove24 >= 4
AND LowestPillarScore >= 16
```

If these conditions are met:

- Blackmere passes the Year 3 Assessment.
- Rupert concedes that the town has demonstrated viable independence.
- The immediate repossession threat is resolved.
- No Year 4 reassessment is scheduled.
- The main story can transition toward autonomy and Future Planning.
- Rupert may still appear later when required by other authored content, but not as a recurring assessment supervisor.

### One Last Chance - Conditional Result

If the Full Pass conditions are not met:

- Blackmere does not immediately lose autonomy.
- Rupert issues the One Last Chance conditional result.
- Day 1 of Spring, Year 4 becomes the reassessment date.
- The player receives targeted covenants generated from the Year 3 pillar scores.
- The covenant interface shows:
    - Year 3 pillar score.
    - Required Year 4 target.
    - Current score.
    - Score-producing actions still available.
    - Whether the covenant has been satisfied.
- The result is never presented as a hidden failure. The player knows exactly which areas Rupert expects to improve.

### Covenant Count

```text
IF TotalAssessmentScore >= 120
    CovenantCount = 2
ELSE
    CovenantCount = 3
```

The system may also issue 3 covenants when the player has three or more pillars below 16, even if the total score is 120 or higher.

### Covenant Selection

Covenants are selected using the following order:

1. Identify every pillar below 24 points.
2. Sort those pillars from lowest score to highest score.
3. Select the lowest-scoring pillars until the required Covenant Count is reached.
4. If fewer qualifying pillars exist than the required Covenant Count, fill the remaining covenant slots using the next-lowest pillar scores.
5. Ties are resolved by selecting the pillar with the greatest number of currently available, unfinished scoring opportunities so the player always has a practical recovery route.

### Covenant Target

Each covenant receives a pillar-score target:

```text
CovenantTarget = Clamp(
    Max(24, Year3PillarScore + 6),
    24,
    32
)
```

Examples:

| Year 3 Pillar Score | Year 4 Covenant Target |
|---:|---:|
| 8 | 24 |
| 16 | 24 |
| 20 | 26 |
| 24 | 30 |
| 28 | 32 |

- A covenant does not force one exact checklist item unless required by story content.
- The player may satisfy the covenant through any valid evidence belonging to that pillar.
- Rupert's report can recommend specific unfinished actions from that save, but recommendations are not additional hidden requirements.

## Conditional Statements on First Assessment

```text
IF TotalAssessmentScore >= 140
AND PillarsAtOrAbove24 >= 4
AND LowestPillarScore >= 16
{
    Result = FullPass
    RepossessionThreatResolved = true
    ScheduleYear4Assessment = false
    CovenantCount = 0
    UnlockFuturePlanningStory = true
}
ELSE
{
    Result = OneLastChance
    RepossessionThreatResolved = false
    ScheduleYear4Assessment = true

    IF TotalAssessmentScore >= 120
    {
        CovenantCount = 2
    }
    ELSE
    {
        CovenantCount = 3
    }

    IF PillarsBelow16 >= 3
    {
        CovenantCount = 3
    }

    GenerateCovenantsFromWeakestPillars(CovenantCount)
}
```

## Year 4 Reassessment

- Rupert returns on Day 1 of Spring, Year 4 only if the Year 3 result was One Last Chance.
- The Year 4 reassessment is not a second identical Year 3 score check.
- Rupert primarily checks:
    - The exact covenants issued after the Year 3 assessment.
    - Whether Blackmere has maintained a minimum overall level of viability.
    - Whether pillars that were previously stable have substantially collapsed.
- The five pillar scores are recalculated from the current save so the player can see the town's present state.

### Stable Pillar Definition

A pillar is considered previously stable when it scored at least 24 points during the Year 3 assessment.

A previously stable pillar is considered to have substantially collapsed when either is true:

```text
CurrentPillarScore < 20
OR
CurrentPillarScore <= Year3PillarScore - 10
```

Only non-covenant pillars use the collapse check. Covenant pillars are judged by their covenant targets.

### Year 4 Pass

Blackmere passes the reassessment when all of the following are true:

```text
AllCovenantsCompleted == true
AND TotalAssessmentScore >= 120
AND SubstantiallyCollapsedStablePillars == 0
```

If these conditions are met:

- The town passes the Year 4 reassessment.
- Rupert concedes that Blackmere has demonstrated sufficient recovery and adaptability.
- The immediate repossession threat is permanently resolved.
- No Regency Tax or assessment oversight is applied.
- The main story transitions toward autonomy and Future Planning.

### Year 4 Failure / Restrictive Continuation

If any Year 4 pass condition is not met:

- Blackmere fails the formal reassessment.
- The save continues normally.
- No restoration state, relationship, invention, collection, or story progress is erased.
- Rupert leaves Blackmere, but the Regency places the town under temporary economic oversight.
- The Regency Tax becomes active:
    - Shops that sell goods or products add a 35% Regency Tax to the player's purchase price.
    - The tax does not reduce the amount the player receives when selling goods.
    - The tax remains active until Blackmere completes the post-assessment independence requirements.
- The game creates an Independence Plan from:
    - Any unfinished Year 4 covenants.
    - Any pillar that substantially collapsed.
    - Any additional pillar below 24 points.
- Once every Independence Plan requirement is completed and the town again reaches at least 140 total assessment points with all five pillars at 24 or higher, a Town Hall story event becomes available to remove Regency oversight and restore full autonomy.
- The player does not need to wait for another fixed annual assessment date after Year 4.

## Conditional Statements on Second Assessment

```text
IF AllCovenantsCompleted == true
AND TotalAssessmentScore >= 120
AND SubstantiallyCollapsedStablePillars == 0
{
    Result = ReassessmentPass
    RepossessionThreatResolved = true
    RegencyOversight = false
    RegencyTaxActive = false
    UnlockFuturePlanningStory = true
}
ELSE
{
    Result = ReassessmentFailed
    RepossessionThreatResolved = false
    RegencyOversight = true
    RegencyTaxActive = true
    RegencyTaxRate = 0.35

    GenerateIndependencePlan(
        UnfinishedCovenants,
        CollapsedStablePillars,
        PillarsBelow24
    )
}
```

## Post-Year 4 Independence Check

After a failed Year 4 reassessment, the game continually tracks the visible Independence Plan.

```text
IF IndependencePlanCompleted == true
AND TotalAssessmentScore >= 140
AND LowestPillarScore >= 24
{
    UnlockIndependencePetitionEvent = true
}
```

Completing the Independence Petition event:

- Resolves the repossession threat.
- Removes the Regency Tax.
- Removes Regency oversight.
- Preserves all player progress.
- Unlocks the autonomy/Future Planning story state.

## Assessment Inspection Route

Rupert's Year 3 and Year 4 visits should visually demonstrate the score rather than presenting only a results screen.

### Year 3 Route Selection

The inspection selects approximately four to six stops:

- One Town Hall / civic anchor.
- One location representing the strongest pillar.
- Two locations representing the weakest pillars.
- One location demonstrating a major installed invention, synergy, trade connection, or community activity when available.
- One optional tie-breaker location based on the save's most unusual or noteworthy evidence.

### Year 4 Route Selection

The reassessment prioritizes:

- Locations directly connected to issued covenants.
- Locations showing whether previously stable systems remained functional.
- A final Town Hall results event.

### Inspection Rules

- The inspection route does not modify scores.
- Rupert's dialogue references actual scoring evidence.
- A Weathered or underused location can be deliberately selected when it represents a weak pillar.
- A high-stage location should be shown in active use when possible rather than treated as a static backdrop.
- NPC routines, shop services, crowds, installed inventions, and public activities may be temporarily anchored to the inspection event when necessary for presentation, but the event should reflect genuine unlocked states.

## Design Contract

- The player receives two full years before the first formal assessment.
- The maximum assessment score is 200.
- Each of the five pillars is capped at 40 points.
- No single pillar must be maximized.
- More evidence exists within each pillar than is necessary to reach the cap.
- Different viable playstyles can pass.
- The Year 3 pass threshold is visible.
- Year 3 failures become targeted One Last Chance covenants rather than immediate repossession.
- Year 4 evaluates the exact covenants plus continued baseline stability.
- Relationship requirements never force romance, marriage, or children.
- Winning the Grand Showcase is never mandatory.
- Completing every Library or Hall of Wonder donation is never mandatory.
- Fully repaying every loan is never mandatory if the economy is otherwise viable.
- No single restoration order is required.
- Failure never deletes or invalidates the save.
- The player can recover from a failed Year 4 reassessment.
- Post-assessment gameplay continues indefinitely.
